One actuator, many callers

Eight explorers sit on a ring and their perception health decays, partly through hidden shocks. A single actuator drives around the ring and restores one explorer at a time, so every dispatch costs travel time. Each explorer reports a noisy urgency and reporting is free, which lets a self-interested explorer inflate its claim. The dispatcher can ignore reports, trust them, or audit them against the truth it sees on arrival or at dispatch, and this demo lets you watch how those choices play out.

How to read the ring

  • Explorer: colour and size show its true health (small and red is low, large and blue is high); a dark outline means health is zero.
  • Left bar: true urgency. Right bar: the claim the dispatcher receives.
  • ! Inflater: adds b to its claim, always, only while the actuator is choosing a target, or only while the actuator is far away.
  • Actuator, with a dashed line to its target and an arc showing service progress.

The simulation is a simplified JavaScript port of the repository’s v2 model (ring of 100, speed 1, service of 5 steps, linear decay plus hidden Poisson shocks, autocorrelated report noise, no preemption). It illustrates the mechanisms; the quantitative results are in the paper and README.
